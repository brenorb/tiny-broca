# tiny-broca v1

v1 fixes what the v0 audit found (`docs/v0-audit.md`). The architecture, the
two voices, the teacher and the 58-symbol letter inventory are unchanged, so
v1 is a fine-tune of v0's full training state (step 118,000) on corrected and
extended data.

## What changes

1. **Front end version 2** (`src/tiny_broca/text.py`). The normaliser now
   reads what a person reads aloud and never emits `<unk>`:
   symbols and math (`4 > 3` → "quatro maior que três"), partial, ISO and
   month/year dates (`14/03` → "quatorze de março", day 1 → "primeiro"),
   hours (`14h`, `14h30`, "às duas"), ordinals (`1º`, `2ª`), money in reais,
   dollars, euros and pounds (cents alone, "mil", "milhões de reais"), decimals
   in percentages and temperatures, negatives, ranges, fractions, units, phone
   numbers, CEP, CPF, CNPJ, plates, URLs, e-mails, handles, hashtags,
   abbreviations, chat abbreviations, laughter, roman numerals in context, and
   gender agreement of numbers ("duas horas", "duzentas pessoas", "vinte e uma
   mil"). Acronyms are spelled with Portuguese letter names ("CPF" → "cê pê
   éfe") or read as words when Portuguese can pronounce them (NASA, FIFA),
   from a lexicon of 125 common Brazilian acronyms plus a rule. English
   loanwords and brands are respelled the way Brazilians say them (948
   entries: "online" → "onlaine", "shampoo" → "xampu", "WhatsApp" →
   "uotsápe"). The table comes from checking every word of the corpus against
   the macOS pt-BR and English dictionaries, plus the common vocabulary of
   tech, finance, brands, food and sport.
   The table is the only rule: a word is respelled when it is a table entry
   or an entry + "s". No ending identifies English ("felizes", "peixes",
   "ganaches" and "osmoses" end like "coaches"), so plurals in -es are entries
   of their own. A word that is also Portuguese is not an entry by itself and
   appears only inside a phrase that settles it: "date" is the verb datar in
   "date e assine" and the English noun in "marcou um date"; "set" is September
   in "set/2026" and a tennis set in "no segundo set". Turning Portuguese into
   another word is worse than leaving a loanword unrespelled, so some loanwords
   stay as written ("O date foi ótimo", "Que nice"). Adversarial reviews
   checked this rule before it was used, the last one by a reviewer that had
   not seen the earlier rounds.
   Month abbreviations are expanded only where something marks a date: a slash
   ("8/dez", "set/2026"), a day and a year ("21 set 2025"), or, for the months
   that are not Portuguese words, a day or a period ("15 de out", "em ago.").
   "mar" and "dez" are also the sea and ten, so without a slash they need a
   year or "dia" ("dia 8 de dez"); "set" needs its period ("15 de set.").
   A number from 1900 to 2099 is read as a year, never in the feminine, only
   where something marks a date ("Em 2023 ela mudou", "em março de 2020", "De
   1930 a 1945 Vargas governou"). Anything else still agrees with the next
   word, because a count can have its noun earlier in the sentence ("Das 2000
   vagas, 1500 são..."). Ambiguous phrasing and years before 1900 stay as they
   were: "Em 2000 escolas fecharam", "Em 1500 uma esquadra chegou".
   The stress feature now comes from a Portuguese syllabifier: oxytone endings
   (aqui, caju, rapaz, jardim, comum, reais), hiatus (ra-iz, ju-iz, sa-í-da,
   ra-i-nha), qu/gu onsets, and unstressed function words.
2. **The teacher reads the spoken form.** `generate_audio.py --teacher-text
   spoken` gives Qwen3-TTS `spoken_form(text)`, the exact words of the
   student's tokens, and quality control compares spoken forms without masking
   numbers (`spoken_normalize`). Clips the first ASR rejects get a second
   opinion from parakeet (`rescue_clips.py`); forced alignment is the third
   check.
3. **Data.**
   * batch5: the 2,800 v0 sentences whose spoken form differs from their
     written form (every number, symbol, acronym and loanword sentence), plus
     the template sentences with grammar errors (corrected), regenerated; the
     superseded v0 clips are dropped.
   * batch4: 4,500 new sentences for the coverage gaps: loanwords, money with
     "reais" in every position, long numbers and identifiers, dates, times,
     symbols and math, units, ordinals, acronyms, abbreviations, roman
     numerals, gender agreement, URLs, stress-rule words, questions and
     exclamations.
4. **Switched excitation.** The harmonic branch of the waveform head is
   excited with noise instead of pulses in unvoiced frames, in training and at
   inference (`--switched-excitation`; the inference checkpoint records it and
   the ONNX graph follows it). v0's pulse train never stopped, so unvoiced
   sounds were partly built out of pulses.
5. **Evaluation.** The 287-sentence probe suite of the audit (29 categories,
   both voices, two ASRs) plus `evaluate.py` with an unmasked spoken-form WER
   and 25 samples per voice and split.

Reproduce: `scripts/build_v1_corpus.py`, `scripts/generate_audio.py
--batches 4 5 --teacher-text spoken --out data/audio-v1`,
`scripts/rescue_clips.py --audio data/audio-v1`, `scripts/v1_pipeline.sh`.

The front end alone took v0 from 242 to 43 missed keywords; the re-read data,
the extra training and the switched excitation took it to 18. Of the 18, 8 are
roman numerals that the transcribers write as numerals ("Dom Pedro II" for
"segundo"). 2 are "CEO", whose table entry said "ci ai ôu" (C-I-O); it is
fixed, and both voices are now heard as "CEO". The rest: "Copa XX" read as
letters (Copa is not a roman-numeral context), "Paraty" (the final y is read
as e; fixed in v1.1), "Itu" (merged into "urubudito"), "story" (heard as "história") and
"crush". The weakest categories by word error are web addresses (19.0%),
roman numerals (14.1%) and tongue twisters (13.8%).

**Held-out sentences** (`evaluate.py`, Whisper small, spoken-form word error
with numbers counted). On the 16 novel sentences v0 had 8.1% and v1 has 1.5%.
On the test split v1 has 2.1% and the teacher's own recordings 2.8%, so the
student is as intelligible as its data. The v0 report masked numbers, so its
transcripts were rescored with the v1 metric.

**"reais", which v0 swallowed.** In `O café custou R$3,00.`, v0 said the
word in about 300 ms, 12 dB below the rest of the sentence in F1 (5 dB in
M1), with faint formants. v1 takes about 600 ms at 4 to 5 dB below, the usual
drop at the end of a sentence, and the final s is there
([spectrograms](img/reais_v0_v1.png), shaded).

**Listening.** `scripts/compare_samples.py` writes a v0 × v1 page with 21
sentences in both voices. Its transcripts show v1 reading all four reported
problems correctly ("online", `4 > 3`, `R$3,00`, `14/03`).

**Web demo.** The page runs the v1 ONNX and front end unchanged; checked
locally, the four reported sentences give 13.7 s of audio in 0.38 s.

## Decisions

### Loanwords: a respelling lexicon, not a bilingual model

In a Brazilian Portuguese sentence, "online", "shampoo", "Bitcoin" and
"tradeoff" are said the Brazilian way ("onláini", "xampu", "bitecóin",
"treidófi"), with Portuguese vowels, an epenthetic final "i" and Portuguese
stress, which ordinary Portuguese spelling can write down. A respelling table
therefore gives the right target, needs no new symbols and no extra capacity,
and works with the letter-based model as it is. It has direct precedent in
other ports of the same architecture (a 462-entry table in the Korean port, a
121-entry pt-BR loanword table in SemFence's phonemiser).

A bilingual model would read English words with English phonology, which is
not how they are said in Portuguese, and it needs what a letter model cannot
provide: English letters do not map to sounds by rule (the English original
uses a CMUdict-based phonemiser for this reason), a per-word language decision
(the same lookup problem as the lexicon), and an English corpus in the same
two voices. That is a v2 project with a phoneme front end (see below).

Validation: fed the respelled sentences, the teacher's audio is transcribed by
parakeet as the English words ("onlaine" → "online", "xampu" → "shampoo",
"uotsápe" → "WhatsApp", "treidófe" → "trade-off"), and v0's own CTC letters
show the respellings fix its readings (raw "hacker" → *aquele*, respelled →
correct).

### No weight editing ("Astra")

The method behind the "Astra" suggestion is MIT CSAIL's *Sparks of
Weight-Space Programming* (GPT-6 Astra reading and editing weights). Its
authors conclude that edits remove obstacles from a model but do not add
abilities it lacks. The Alpa (Sanskrit) port of tiny-voca edited only the
English phoneme and stress tables by nearest-phoneme lookup (0.2% of the
parameters) and then trained on 3.5 h of speech; the training taught the
language. For tiny-broca, editing the English tiny-voca into Portuguese would
only produce a starting point we already have (our own v0, with its full
training state including the discriminators; tiny-voca publishes inference
weights only, with a 76-phoneme inventory that does not match our letters),
and editing v0 to add English cannot copy tiny-voca's rows, because the two
embedding spaces are unrelated. Not used in v1.

### Emotion and nonverbal sounds: v2

The fiato port shows the recipe (`<sigh>`, `<exhale>`, `<gasp>` tokens that own
the sound between words, from a teacher that renders them). No teacher we can
use renders them in Brazilian Portuguese: OmniVoice's weights are
non-commercial, Fish Audio S2 Pro's licence makes any model trained on its
output a derivative, KittenTTS forbids training on its output, and the
permissive Portuguese teachers (Qwen3-TTS, Chatterbox Multilingual, Kokoro)
have no inline tags. Splicing English sighs into Portuguese clips is possible
but needs the switched excitation first (breaths are unvoiced, exactly what
v0's pulse train rendered badly). The symbol inventory is append-only, so tag
tokens can be added in v2 without breaking v1 checkpoints.

### Teacher

Qwen3-TTS 1.7B Base stays: Apache-2.0, clones the two existing voices, and
reads Portuguese correctly once it is given spoken words. The v0 failures that
looked like teacher failures were the teacher being handed digits and symbols.

## v1.1

v1.1 is a front-end release on the v1 model. `text.py` changes; the weights,
the ONNX file and the dataset do not.

**y reads as i.** Portuguese spelling writes y as i (Parati, Ipiranga,
iogurte), and the model had barely seen the letter. v1 read a final y as e
("Paraty" was heard as "Parate", "Itamaraty" as "Itamarate") and a starting y
before a vowel as j ("Yuri" as "júri", "Yan" as "Jean"). After the loanword
table, which respells the English words that say it otherwise (byte →
"báite", Tyson → "táisson"), every y becomes i. "yoga" left the table: the
rule gives "ioga". A lone y is the letter, "ípsilon" (`eixo y`, `f(x, y)`,
`y = 2x + 1`), except a lower-case y between capitalised words, which is the
Spanish conjunction (`Ortega y Gasset` → "ortega i gasset").

As for every text rule here, adversarial reviewers attacked it before it was
applied. The first found the Spanish conjunction, and that a starting y turned
into i adds a syllable in some names. An A/B test on v1's audio settled that
case for i (Yuri and Yan above). The final review, by a reviewer that had not
seen the first, found that `_cleanup` folded ý and ỹ back into y after the
rule (the rule now runs after it) and that a lone y had become i where it is
the letter.

**131 new respellings** cover the crypto, AI and tech words Brazilians don't
translate (exchange, seed phrase, halving, node, Lightning Network, satoshi,
HODL, DeFi, smart contract, mempool, Taproot, Nostr, Cashu; Llama, Grok,
PyTorch, Hugging Face, fine-tune, vibe coding; Bluesky, Substack), English
names (Daisy → "dêizi", Taylor → "têilor", Mike Tyson → "máique táisson") and
English words whose y says "ai" (style, cyber, sky). Each was spoken by the v1
model in both voices and transcribed with parakeet. 96 of the 130 tested came
back as the English term; 33 came back as a phonetic spelling of it from a
transcriber that does not know the word ("Satoxi", "Halvin", "Nóster").
Cashu was recognised in neither form tested and is read "cachú", as the
maintainer says it. The table has 1,078 loanwords and 127 acronyms.

The release changes 41 of the corpus's 15,022 sentences, all of them names or
English words (Itamaraty, Stefany, Niemeyer, academy, Excel). Names stressed
on the last syllable ("Lucy" read "lu-CÍ") are unchanged: the spelling cannot
tell them from Paraty or Itamaraty, which are stressed there.

## v2 ideas

  dictionary (CC BY 4.0, ~33k words) plus rules, which would also open a
  bilingual PT/EN model with an English phonemiser.
* Nonverbal tags (above), question intonation data, more voices.
